Agora's answer:

> Hobbes lists four "Abuses" of speech, and the first is the one by which people deceive themselves. It happens when men "register their thoughts wrong" because the meaning of their words keeps shifting (the "inconstancy of the signification" of words). They then record as their conceptions things they never conceived, and so deceive themselves (Thomas Hobbes, Leviathan, Part I, Chapter 4, Abuses Of Speech, paragraph 4 (edition-specific)). By contrast, Hobbes says the second abuse, using words metaphorically (in a sense other than the one they were ordained for), deceives others rather than oneself (Thomas Hobbes, Leviathan, Part I, Chapter 4, Abuses Of Speech, paragraph 4 (edition-specific)).
